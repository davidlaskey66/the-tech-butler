Ring or Nest Doorbell Battery Dying in the Cold? Here’s How to Fix It

Published November 10, 2026 • 7 min read

If your Ring or Nest video doorbell battery is dying fast, or the app says it isn't charging, now that the weather has turned cold, the cold is almost certainly the cause. Both brands say their batteries stop charging below freezing and drain faster in the cold. That stays true even when the doorbell is wired to your home. The fix is usually one of three things: cut down how much power the doorbell uses, charge the battery indoors, or switch to a doorbell that doesn't rely on a battery at all. For homeowners in Collingwood, Blue Mountains, Wasaga Beach, this is one of the most common doorbell problems we see once winter arrives.

Here's why it happens and how to fix it, starting with the easiest steps.

Why Does My Doorbell Battery Die in Cold Weather?

Video doorbells use rechargeable lithium-ion batteries, the same basic chemistry as your phone. Cold slows the chemical reactions inside the battery, so it holds and delivers less energy. Charging a lithium-ion battery below freezing can also damage it, so manufacturers build in a cut-off.

The numbers from the manufacturers:

  • Ring says its devices operate normally between -20°C and 50°C, that battery life will be shorter around freezing, and that the device "is designed to stop charging if the temperature is below freezing."
  • Google Nest has said its battery doorbell keeps working down to -20°C but won't charge below 0°C, and that near -20°C the battery may last less than half as long as it does in warm weather. The Google Home app may show "Charging paused" or "Charging slowly" while it's too cold.

So in a typical Ontario winter, a battery doorbell can go weeks without taking any charge while still working hard: recording motion, holding a Wi-Fi connection and running night vision through long, dark evenings. The battery only goes one way.

"But My Doorbell Is Hardwired. Why Is the Battery Still Draining?"

Many people are surprised by this. Battery doorbells that you can connect to existing doorbell wires use that power to top up the battery. The camera still runs off the battery. Google has confirmed that a wired Nest Doorbell (battery) still drains in freezing weather because the battery can't charge, and that if it drains completely the doorbell shuts down until you bring it inside to charge. Ring owners with wired battery models report the same pattern on Ring's community forums every winter.

A weak or undersized doorbell transformer makes it worse. Many older homes have a transformer that was sized for a simple mechanical chime, not a camera. Even in mild weather it may not supply enough power to keep up with a busy doorbell.

Step 1: Cut Down How Much Power the Doorbell Uses

This costs nothing and often buys you weeks. In the Ring app, open the menu (☰), tap Devices, choose your doorbell and tap Power Settings. Ring shows the battery level there and which features are using extra power, and you can tap a feature to adjust it.

Ring's own advice for a battery that drains fast is to:

  • Lower the sensitivity in Motion Settings.
  • Tighten your Motion Zones so passing cars and the street aren't covered.
  • Set Motion Frequency to Regularly or Periodically instead of the most frequent setting.
  • Use Live View less often, and unlink the doorbell from other cameras so they don't trigger it to record.

On a Nest doorbell, the same ideas apply in the Google Home app: fewer events, smaller activity zones and fewer live-view check-ins all mean less drain. Menu names in the Home app change often, so look in the doorbell's settings for its event and activity-zone options.

Snow is worth checking too. Snow drifting past the camera, or a driveway light blinking on and off, can trigger dozens of recordings a night.

Step 2: Check Your Wi-Fi Signal at the Front Door

A doorbell with a weak Wi-Fi signal works harder to stay connected, and that drains the battery. Ring lists poor Wi-Fi as one of the main causes of fast drain. You can check the signal strength (shown as RSSI) under Device Health in the Ring app.

This is very common locally. The Bell or Rogers modem is often in a basement or back office, and the front door is the far end of the house, sometimes through brick or stone. If the signal is weak, a mesh Wi-Fi point near the front entry often helps the battery as much as any setting change. Step 3: Charge the Battery Indoors (and Let It Warm Up First)

Ring recommends bringing the battery inside to charge with a USB cable during cold spells rather than waiting for a solar panel to charge it. Ring says a full charge can take up to 10 hours. Many Ring battery doorbells use a removable battery pack. If yours does, the easiest routine in winter is to keep a second pack charged indoors and swap them whenever the app warns you the battery is low.

Google says a Nest battery only starts charging once it's back to 0°C or warmer. Its battery is built in, so the whole doorbell has to come off its mount and come inside. Give it time to warm up to room temperature before plugging it in. If it shows "Charging paused" at first, that usually just means the battery is still cold.

Solar panels can't fix this in December. Days are short, panels get covered in snow, and the battery won't take a charge below freezing even when the sun is out.

Step 4: Check the Doorbell Transformer and Wiring

If your doorbell is wired but drains even when temperatures are above freezing, the power supply is the next suspect. Signs include the app reporting poor power, the chime buzzing or not ringing, or the battery falling steadily on mild days. The doorbell transformer is usually near the electrical panel, the furnace, or inside the chime box. It needs to supply the voltage your doorbell model requires, and some setups also need the right chime type set in the app or a power kit installed at the chime.

Testing and replacing a transformer involves line-voltage electrical work. If you're not comfortable with that, this is the point to call in help.

Step 5: Switch to a Wired-Only Doorbell

For a front door that faces long Ontario winters, the permanent fix is a doorbell that runs entirely on house wiring and has no battery to drain. Ring sells several wired-only models, and Google makes a wired Nest Doorbell. These don't stop working in the cold the way battery models do, and you never have to take them down to charge.

You need working low-voltage doorbell wiring and an adequate transformer at the front door. Most homes in the area have doorbell wiring, though older wiring and transformers sometimes need to be upgraded. How to Prevent It Next Winter

  • Before the first hard frost, charge the battery to full indoors and set conservative motion settings.
  • Turn on low-battery notifications so the doorbell doesn't die without warning.
  • Keep a spare battery pack charged if your model takes one.
  • If the doorbell sits on an exposed, north-facing or lakeside wall, plan on a wired-only model.
  • For a seasonal property or second home that sits empty for weeks, nobody is there to bring a battery inside. Go wired, or expect the doorbell to be offline when you need it most.

When to Call a Professional

Call for help if the doorbell is wired but still drains in mild weather, if the chime has stopped working, if you'd need to replace a transformer or run new wire, or if the doorbell is at a property you aren't at often enough to manage batteries. These are electrical and network problems, not app settings, and guessing at them usually takes more time than it saves.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Ring doorbells work in cold weather?

Yes. Ring says its devices operate normally down to -20°C. Below freezing, though, the battery drains faster and won't charge, so battery models need more attention in winter.

Why does my hardwired Ring or Nest doorbell say the battery is low?

On battery models, the wiring only recharges the battery, and the battery can't charge below freezing. In mild weather, a low battery on a wired doorbell usually points to a weak transformer, wiring problem or very high motion activity.

At what temperature does a Nest doorbell stop charging?

Google has said the Nest Doorbell (battery) won't charge below 0°C, though it keeps working down to -20°C. Once it's back above 0°C, charging can resume.

Should I bring my doorbell inside in the winter?

Only to charge it. Bring the battery or doorbell inside when it's low, let it warm up, charge it fully and put it back. If you're doing this often, a wired-only doorbell is the better long-term fix.
