Water Leak Detectors and Automatic Shut-Off Valves: What Works

Published March 18, 2026 • Updated November 4, 2026 • 6 min read

The best protection against water damage at a home that sits empty is a leak sensor that alerts your phone, combined with an automatic shut-off valve that closes the main water line the moment a leak is detected. Sensors alone tell you there's a problem. A shut-off valve stops it. At a property nobody will check for a week, that difference can decide whether you have a wet floor or a gutted basement.

This guide covers the three types of systems, where to put sensors, which products are available in Canada, what they cost, and the mistakes that leave owners unprotected without realizing it.

Why Leaks Do So Much Damage at Empty Properties

In an occupied home, someone usually notices a leak within hours. At a vacation property, a second home or a house that sits empty over the winter, a slow drip under a sink or a failed water heater can run for days. By the time anyone arrives, water has soaked the subfloor and drywall, and mould may already be growing.

Ontario winters add a second risk. If the furnace fails or the power goes out for long enough, pipes can freeze and burst. When they thaw, water flows at full pressure until someone shuts the main off.

The Three Types of Leak Protection

1. Spot leak sensors (alert only)

These are small battery-powered sensors that sit on the floor next to anything that can leak. When water touches the contacts, the sensor sounds a local alarm and, if it's connected to Wi-Fi or a hub, sends an alert to your phone. They're inexpensive and you can install them yourself in minutes, but they only tell you about the problem. Someone still has to get there and turn off the water.

2. Sensor-triggered shut-off valves

A motorized valve on the main water line connects to a set of leak sensors. When any sensor detects water, the valve closes automatically. Sinopé's Sedna, designed in Canada, works this way: the valve supports up to 20 leak detectors and closes within seconds of a detection. It can also close the water locally even when the internet is down, though in that case you won't get the notification.

3. Whole-home flow monitors with shut-off

These install on the main line and watch the water itself: flow, pressure and temperature. Instead of waiting for water to reach a sensor, they spot abnormal usage, such as water running continuously when no one is home, and shut off the supply. Moen's Flo Smart Water Monitor and Shutoff falls in this category. It runs a daily test for small drips and also watches for high pressure and freezing conditions. Flow monitoring catches leaks that spot sensors miss, such as a pipe leaking inside a wall or ceiling.

Where to Place Leak Sensors

Put a sensor anywhere water is supplied or stored. The most important spots are:

  • The water heater: tanks fail with age and can release a lot of water quickly.
  • The washing machine: supply hoses are one of the most common sources of sudden, large leaks.
  • Under the kitchen and bathroom sinks
  • Behind the dishwasher and the fridge: fridges with an ice maker or water line are a common slow-leak source.
  • The sump pit: a sensor here warns you if the pump fails.
  • Near the furnace and any humidifier, where condensate lines can clog and overflow.
  • The main water entry and shut-off area in the basement or utility room.
  • Beside toilets, especially in bathrooms that rarely get used.

Place sensors flat on the floor at the lowest point, where water will pool first. Many models offer a sensing cable, which covers more floor than a single puck under long appliances or along a basement wall.

Five Mistakes That Leave You Unprotected

Relying only on Wi-Fi

Most sensors send alerts over your home network. If the power or internet goes out during a storm, a common time for problems, the alerts stop. The local alarm still sounds, but nobody's there to hear it. Choose a valve that can close locally without the internet, consider a battery backup for your router and modem, and pick sensors and valves with backup power. Sedna, for example, offers optional battery backup.

Letting batteries die

A sensor with a dead battery offers no protection. Check battery status in the app at the start of each season, and replace batteries on a schedule rather than waiting for a warning.

Sending alerts only to yourself

If you're three hours away, an alert at 2 AM doesn't help unless someone nearby can respond. Add a caretaker, neighbour or property manager to the app so they get alerts too.

Ignoring freeze warnings

Leak sensors react after water escapes. A low-temperature alert gives you warning before a pipe freezes. Many leak sensors also report temperature, and a smart thermostat can send a low-temperature alert. Set it high enough to leave time to act, around 10–12°C at an empty property.

Never testing the system

Test each sensor by touching its contacts with a damp cloth, and confirm the alert reaches every phone on the account. If you have a shut-off valve, close it from the app and make sure the water actually stops.

What It Costs in Canada

Prices at the time of writing; check current pricing before you buy:

  • Basic Wi-Fi spot sensors are the least expensive option and are widely sold at Canadian retailers, often in multi-packs.
  • Sinopé Sedna smart water valve: listed by Sinopé at $279.99 for the 3/4" versions and $299.99 for the 1" version. Leak detectors and battery backup are sold separately.
  • Moen Flo Smart Water Monitor and Shutoff (3/4"): listed at $799 at Home Depot Canada.

Shut-off valves and flow monitors install on your main water line, and both manufacturers call for professional installation, so budget for that on top of the hardware.

Can It Lower Your Home Insurance?

Sometimes. Some Canadian insurers offer discounts for water-protection devices. The Personal, for example, advertises up to 10% off the basic home insurance premium for a shut-off valve paired with leak detectors, and up to 5% for leak detectors connected to a central monitoring station, subject to conditions. Discounts and eligibility vary by insurer and policy, so ask yours what qualifies before you buy, and keep proof of installation.

Which System Is Right for You?

  • A home you live in full time: spot sensors in the key locations are a sensible minimum.
  • A vacation property, second home or short-term rental: pair leak sensors with an automatic shut-off valve. An alert you can't act on doesn't prevent damage.
  • A larger home, or one with plumbing in finished walls and ceilings: a whole-home flow monitor catches hidden leaks that spot sensors can't.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do water leak sensors work without Wi-Fi?

Most sound a local alarm without Wi-Fi, but they can't send alerts to your phone. Some shut-off valves can still close the water automatically when the internet is down.

What is the difference between a leak sensor and an automatic shut-off valve?

A leak sensor detects water and alerts you. An automatic shut-off valve is installed on the main water line and closes it when a leak is detected, which stops the damage instead of just reporting it.

Where should I put water leak sensors?

Next to the water heater, washing machine, sinks, dishwasher, fridge water line, sump pit, furnace and humidifier, toilets and the main water entry. Put them flat on the floor at the lowest point.

Can a smart water shut-off valve prevent frozen pipe damage?

It can limit the damage by closing the main line when a burst pipe starts leaking, and some flow monitors also warn about freezing conditions. A low-temperature alert from a sensor or thermostat gives you the earliest warning.

Do I need a plumber to install a water shut-off valve?

Valves that install on the main water line should be installed professionally according to local building codes. Spot leak sensors need no installation beyond placing them and connecting them to the app.
