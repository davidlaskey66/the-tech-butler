Spring Smart Home Checklist for Vacation Properties

Published March 12, 2026 • Updated November 4, 2026 • 5 min read

After a long Ontario winter, the technology at a vacation property is often in worse shape than the owner realizes. A power outage can reset the router, cold weather drains smart lock batteries, and a winter storm can knock a camera out of position. Any of these can leave the property unmonitored for weeks without anyone noticing.

This checklist covers every system worth checking before your first visit or booking of the season. Work through it from top to bottom: the network comes first because everything else depends on it. Most owners can finish it in about an hour.

1. Wi-Fi and Internet: Check This First

Every smart device at the property depends on the network, so start here. Open your router or mesh Wi-Fi app and confirm every node is online with a normal status.

  • Look for a factory reset. A power outage or surge over the winter can reset some routers to factory settings, which reverts the network name and password to the defaults. If that happened, every smart device on the network is offline until it's reconnected. This is the most common problem we find at the start of the season.
  • Check the internet service. Run a speed test and confirm your plan is still active. Plans on properties with low winter usage sometimes get changed or suspended.
  • Install firmware updates for the router and every mesh node. This is easy to overlook, and it's important for both security and stability.
  • Reboot the whole system. Restart the main router first, then each node, working outward. This clears memory and applies pending updates.
  • Review connected devices. Remove anything that no longer belongs on the network, and confirm the guest network is on with the password you want for the season.

2. Smart Locks and Access Codes

A lock that doesn't respond or a code that fails is the problem guests notice first, and it usually happens after hours.

  • Replace the batteries now. Cold weather drains lock batteries faster, even ones that were full in the fall. Replace any below about 50% rather than waiting for a low-battery warning during a guest stay.
  • Delete old codes. Remove codes from past guests, contractors and service providers. Keep only your own, your caretaker's and any active bookings.
  • Test every entry point: front door, back door, garage and any secondary doors.
  • Check the physical key so you know the mechanical backup still works.
  • Update the firmware. A lock that was slow or unreliable over the winter often improves after an update, and outdated firmware is one of the most common causes of lock problems.

3. Security Cameras

  • Confirm every camera is online and the live feed loads quickly.
  • Check each camera's angle. Ice, wind, fallen branches and snow on the mounts can shift coverage noticeably over a winter.
  • Clean the lenses. Condensation, dust, pollen and spider webs build up over a closed season and blur the image.
  • Review winter motion alerts. Most will be weather or wildlife, but any activity while the property was supposed to be empty is worth a look.
  • Confirm your recording plan. If you use cloud storage, make sure the subscription is still active.
  • Retune motion sensitivity. Spring brings landscapers, neighbours and wildlife, so adjust zones and sensitivity to cut false alerts.

4. Video Doorbell

Press the button and confirm the chime sounds, the live view loads and two-way audio is clear. Moisture over the winter can affect the speaker and microphone. For battery models, charge or replace the battery. For wired models, a doorbell that keeps dropping offline can point to a weak transformer.

If you narrowed the motion zones for winter to avoid snowplow alerts, reset them for spring and summer traffic.

5. Smart Thermostat

A vacant-winter setting isn't the right setting for a property you're about to use or rent. Set comfortable temperatures for when people are there and an away temperature for empty periods.

Don't turn the heat off yet. Overnight frost is still common in this area well into spring, so keep a minimum of about 12–13°C while the property is empty, and turn on a low-temperature alert so you hear about a furnace problem right away. If your system also cools, confirm it switches between heating and cooling correctly.

For the detailed settings, including schedules, geofencing and freeze protection, see our guide to managing a smart thermostat through the spring transition.

6. Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Detectors

Test every detector with its test button, even if the app shows them as healthy, because a physical test confirms the alarm itself works. Replace low batteries, and check each unit's manufacture date: most manufacturers recommend replacing smoke alarms after 10 years, and CO alarms have a shorter lifespan, often 7 to 10 years depending on the model. The replace-by date is printed on the back of the unit.

7. Lighting Schedules

Sunrise and sunset shift by hours between January and June. Most smart lighting adjusts automatically using your location, but confirm the schedules look right in the app. That is especially true for outdoor lights: a porch light set to come on at 5 PM made sense in winter but wastes power in June.

8. TVs and Streaming Devices

Streaming devices that have been off for months usually need software updates before they work properly, and each one can take 15 to 30 minutes. Power everything on and let the updates finish before guests arrive. Also confirm your streaming logins still work, because a password change on your main account can sign the property's devices out without warning.

9. Final Check: Test Remote Access From Off-Site

Finish this from your phone, ideally while you're away from the property, and confirm you can:

  • Lock and unlock each smart lock remotely
  • Open a live feed from every camera
  • Adjust the thermostat
  • Receive motion and temperature alerts

If something doesn't work remotely, fix it now. That's much easier than dealing with it at 10 PM with a guest locked out.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I check my vacation property's technology in spring?

Do it before your first visit or booking of the season, ideally two to three weeks ahead, so there's time to order parts or book a service visit if something is wrong.

Why are my smart devices offline after the winter?

The most common cause is a router that reset itself after a power outage, which changes the network name and password. Dead batteries and missed firmware updates are the next most common causes.

Should I turn off the heat at my vacation property in spring?

Not while overnight frost is still possible. Keep a minimum of about 12–13°C while the property is empty and use a low-temperature alert until the nights are reliably above freezing.

How often should smart lock batteries be replaced?

It depends on the lock and how much it's used, but cold weather shortens battery life considerably. Replacing them at the start of each season is the simplest way to avoid a lockout.
