Smart Lock Not Working in Cold Weather? A Canadian Winter Fix Guide

Published February 3, 2026 • Updated November 17, 2026 • 7 min read

If your smart lock started acting up when the temperature dropped, you're not alone. Quality smart locks are built to handle a Canadian winter, but cold weather exposes four weak spots: tired batteries, a deadbolt that binds as the door shifts, ice on the keypad, and a weak Wi-Fi signal at the door. Most of these can be fixed in a few minutes once you know which one you're dealing with. This guide walks through each fix, easiest first, for homeowners in Collingwood, Blue Mountains, Wasaga Beach and anywhere else that sees a real Ontario winter.

Do Smart Locks Work in Cold Weather?

Yes, the good ones do, but the ratings are worth understanding. Manufacturers rate the outside and inside of the lock separately:

  • Schlage Encode Plus: the outside of the lock is rated down to -35°C, but the inside lock body is rated only down to -10°C.
  • Yale Assure Lock 2: the outside is rated down to -30°C, the inside down to -15°C, and the batteries down to -10°C.

The batteries and most of the electronics sit on the inside of the door. On a heated house, that's no problem. Trouble starts when the inside of the door gets cold too: an unheated mudroom or vestibule, a service door into an unheated garage, or a seasonal property where the heat has been turned right down. If your lock is on one of those doors, keep that in mind as you work through the fixes below.

Why Smart Locks Stop Working in Winter

  • Battery voltage drops in the cold. Batteries deliver less power when they're cold, and the motor needs a burst of power to throw the bolt. Batteries that were fine in September can fail in January.
  • The deadbolt binds. Doors and frames shift as the temperature swings, and frost builds up on weatherstripping. If the bolt rubs on the strike plate, the motor struggles, reports a jam and burns through batteries.
  • Ice and snow on the keypad. Freezing rain and drifting snow can coat a keypad or fill the keyway.
  • Weak Wi-Fi at the door. Front doors are often the furthest point from a modem tucked in the basement. A lock with a weak signal goes offline, and some locks use more battery power trying to stay connected.

How to Fix a Smart Lock That Stops Working in the Cold

Step 1: Put in fresh batteries of the right type

Start here, even if the app says the batteries are fine. Use a fresh, matched set from the same package and check every battery is facing the right way.

Be careful with the popular advice to switch to lithium batteries for winter. Schlage says its smart locks, including the Encode and Encode Plus, are not compatible with lithium batteries and recommends premium alkaline AAs. Its reason is a good one: lithium cells hold their voltage and then drop off very suddenly, so the lock gives little or no low-battery warning before it dies. Other brands have their own guidance, so check your lock's manual before you change battery types.

Step 2: Test the deadbolt by hand

This is the most overlooked fix. Open the door and lock and unlock it with the thumb turn. The bolt should move smoothly. Now close the door and try again. If it suddenly feels stiff, or you have to push or pull on the door to get it to lock, the bolt is rubbing on the strike plate.

Check for frost or ice on the weatherstripping and the strike plate first. If the door itself has shifted, the strike plate may need adjusting so the bolt slides in without touching the edges. A smart lock motor can't overcome the friction a person can, so a door that "just needs a little shove" is often the whole problem.

Step 3: Clear ice from the keypad and keyway

Brush snow off the keypad and gently warm any ice with your hand or a hair dryer on a low setting. Don't pour hot water on the lock: it refreezes and can work its way inside. Don't spray household oil into the keyway either. Before you put any lubricant or de-icer in a smart lock, check what the manufacturer recommends.

Step 4: Check the Wi-Fi signal at the door

Stand at the door with your phone on Wi-Fi. If your phone's connection is weak or slow there, the lock is struggling too. Many Wi-Fi smart locks connect only on the 2.4 GHz band (Schlage lists a 2.4 GHz network as a requirement for the Encode Plus), which travels further than 5 GHz, but signal still has to get through exterior walls, brick and metal doors.

If the signal is poor, moving the router out of a basement corner or adding a mesh Wi-Fi point near the entry usually solves it. This is a common issue with the modems Bell and Rogers install, simply because they're often placed wherever the cable comes into the house. Our Wi-Fi optimization service covers exactly this.

Step 5: Re-run the lock's setup and calibration in the app

After you've fixed the batteries, the bolt or the Wi-Fi, open the lock's app and check that it shows the correct locked or unlocked status and the latest firmware. Many locks include a calibration or door-orientation step in their settings; re-running it after a strike plate adjustment helps the lock know where "fully locked" is. Menu names vary by brand and app version, so follow the steps in your lock's own app.

Step 6: Rethink the lock on an unheated door

If the lock is on a door where the inside regularly drops well below freezing, no amount of tweaking will change the inside-temperature rating. The real fix is keeping that space above the rated range, choosing a different door for keyless entry, or using hardware suited to that location.

How to Prevent Smart Lock Problems This Winter

  • Replace the batteries every fall before the first cold snap. Schlage recommends replacing them once a year.
  • Turn on low-battery notifications in the app, and make sure they reach a phone someone checks.
  • Keep a physical backup key with someone you trust nearby, never under the mat.
  • At a seasonal property or second home, keep the heat set high enough that the inside of the door stays within the lock's rating.
  • For short-term rentals, change batteries between seasons and test the guest code yourself before a winter check-in date.
  • Check that the door closes and locks smoothly by hand after every big temperature swing.

When to Call a Professional

Call for help if:

  • The lock keeps reporting jams even with fresh batteries
  • The door needs to be pushed or lifted to lock, or the strike plate needs to be moved
  • The lock keeps dropping offline and your Wi-Fi doesn't reach the door
  • You manage the lock remotely for a vacation property or rental and can't afford a lockout
  • The lock is on a door that gets too cold on the inside and you need a different plan

Frequently Asked Questions

Do smart locks work at -30°C?

The outside of many quality smart locks is rated for it. Schlage rates the outside of the Encode Plus to -35°C and Yale rates the outside of the Assure Lock 2 to -30°C. The batteries and electronics on the inside are rated for a narrower range, so the lock works best when the inside of the door stays heated.

Should I use lithium batteries in my smart lock in winter?

Not unless your lock's manufacturer says to. Schlage says its locks are not compatible with lithium batteries and recommends premium alkaline AAs, partly because lithium cells drop off so suddenly that the lock gives little or no low-battery warning. Check your own lock's manual before switching.

Why does my smart lock jam in the winter?

Usually the deadbolt is rubbing on the strike plate because the door or frame has shifted with the cold, or frost has built up on the weatherstripping. The motor can't push the bolt through the extra friction, so the lock reports a jam and drains its batteries trying. Adjusting the strike plate or door usually fixes it.

What happens if my smart lock batteries die in the cold?

The lock stays in whatever position it was in. Keyed models such as the Schlage Encode Plus can still be opened with the physical key, and Yale's key-free Assure Lock 2 models can be powered temporarily with a 9V battery. That's why you should keep a backup key somewhere other than under the mat.
